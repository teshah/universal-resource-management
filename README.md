# Universal Resource Management Framework

A unified systems-level guide to understanding, developing, and allocating human and structural resources across any domain.

---

## 1. The Five Resource Currencies

Every human and organizational task requires an expenditure from a portfolio of five distinct currencies:

| Currency | Nature | Storage / Replenishment | Depletion Dynamic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Energy** | Biological & Somatic | Cyclical (sleep, nutrition, nervous system recovery) | Non-linear; leads to burnout if forced |
| **2. Time** | Strictly Non-Renewable | Zero-sum; 24 hours/day; cannot be banked | Ticks continuously regardless of action |
| **3. Willpower** | Executive & Cognitive | Fragile; rapid depletion under ambiguity/friction | Drained by impulse regulation and context-switching |
| **4. Skills / Knowledge** | Epistemic Capital | Compounding; slow decay; highly leverageable | Upfront barrier that reduces all future operational costs |
| **5. Money** | Fungible & External | Scalable, investable, transferable, and outsourceable | Linear subtraction per expenditure |

## 3. The Seven Universal Allocation Laws

Applicable across biology, computing, finance, operations research, and personal life:

1. **The Theory of Constraints (Liebigâ€™s Law of the Minimum / Amdahl's Law):** System output is dictated solely by its single tightest bottleneck. Optimizing non-bottlenecks yields zero throughput gain.
2. **The Asymmetry Principle (Pareto 80/20 & Power Laws):** Never allocate symmetrically; overweight resources to the vital 20% of inputs that produce 80% of results.
3. **The Law of Slack (Kingmanâ€™s Formula):** Systems operating near 100% capacity suffer exponential latency and brittleness. Always maintain 15â€“25% unallocated buffer.
4. **The Barbell Strategy (Bimodal Allocation):** Pair an ultra-safe 85â€“90% base (zero risk of ruin) with 10â€“15% high-asymmetry, capped-downside exploratory bets.
5. **Sunk Cost Elimination & Opportunity Cost:** Past investments are unrecoverable; evaluate solely on prospective future return. Remember: *Every "yes" is a simultaneous "no" to everything else.*
6. **The Explore vs. Exploit Dilemma:** Balance harvesting known winners against searching for new opportunities (90/10 in stable environments; 70/30 in volatile environments).
7. **Staged Reversibility (Two-Way vs. One-Way Doors):** Move fast on reversible decisions; commit in small, milestone-gated tranches on irreversible decisions.

## 5. The Role of Values (The Objective Function)

Resources are the fuel, allocation principles are the engine, but **values are the Objective Function (GPS)**.
* Values define what "Return on Investment" actually means.
* Values remove guilt and ambiguity from opportunity cost, turning "no" into an act of integrity.
* Alignment with core values lowers friction and cognitive dissonance, reducing the willpower needed to act.
